# Flagship: local testing vs opinion pooling; persistent divergence

## Question
"In network / social epistemology and opinion dynamics, what is known about agents who EVALUATE a shared hypothesis or structural proposal against their OWN model / data (local testing, replication) — as opposed to POOLING opinions or credences (DeGroot / Lehrer–Wagner averaging)? Does local evaluation under heterogeneous priors/structures yield PERSISTENT divergence, polarization, or incommensurability rather than consensus? Cover bounded-confidence models (Hegselmann–Krause), Lehrer–Wagner consensus/aggregation, Bayesian learning with MISSPECIFIED or heterogeneous models (e.g. Acemoglu–Ozdaglar, Fudenberg-style), and any explicit 'evaluate-against-own-model vs pool' distinction."

## Synthesis

The two halves of the paper's flagship claim land very differently against the prior art. (1) **The "pool opinions vs. update on shared data/evidence" distinction is NOT novel — it is the organizing axis of network epistemology and a known result in formal epistemology.** The Bala–Goyal/Zollman bandit framework is built precisely on agents who *run their own experiments, share the results (data), and update via Bayes' rule on that evidence* — and it is routinely and explicitly contrasted with DeGroot / opinion-pooling (averaging stated credences). Formal epistemology has the sharp version of this: linear opinion pooling is *not* externally Bayesian (pooling-then-updating ≠ updating-then-pooling), whereas geometric pooling is the unique rule that commutes with conditionalization. So "agents who update on shared data behave differently from agents who average opinions" is established, named, and theorem-backed. (2) **The claim that local, own-model evaluation under heterogeneity yields *persistent* divergence/polarization rather than consensus is also already formalized** — by Scientific Polarization (O'Connor & Weatherall: Bayesian agents who *distrust evidence from those whose beliefs are far from their own* lock into stable, evidence-proof polarization), by Bohren & Hauser (heterogeneous *misspecified* models give a closed-form criterion for *entrenched disagreement*), by Acemoglu–Ozdaglar-style heterogeneous-prior learning, and at the opinion-dynamics level by Hegselmann–Krause bounded confidence (which fragments into persistent opinion clusters). Lehrer–Wagner is the canonical *pooling/consensus* pole — weighted iterated averaging that provably drives to consensus — and is exactly the foil the paper needs, with a substantial critical literature (zero-weight problem, non-commutativity with revision).

**Verdict: the local-testing-vs-pooling distinction is NOT novel as a distinction, and "heterogeneous local evaluation → persistent divergence" is NOT novel as a phenomenon.** What appears genuinely unclaimed in this literature is the *specific object*: local testing of a **shared STRUCTURAL revision (a Bayes-net edge / generative-structure change), evaluated against each agent's own model**, producing **structural incommensurability** (different learned *structures/wirings*, not just different scalar credences or means). All the precedents operate on credences over a fixed hypothesis space (two bandit arms, a scalar state, a partisan parameter) — they produce divergence in *belief*, not in *model structure*. The paper should (a) cite this lineage and explicitly position pooling-vs-evaluation as inherited, not invented, and (b) stake the novelty narrowly on structure-valued divergence / structural incommensurability, which the prior art does not formalize.

## Claims

### Claim: The "update on shared data/experiments vs. average stated opinions" distinction is the foundational axis of network epistemology, not a new contribution
- **Evidence:** In the Bala–Goyal/Zollman bandit framework, "each agent selects their action based on their belief, obtains evidence from their action, shares their evidence with their neighbors, and updates on the evidence they receive" via Bayes' rule — i.e. agents pool *data* (own + neighbors' sampling results) and condition, which is explicitly distinguished from DeGroot/opinion-pooling of credences. Studies even compare the two regimes head-to-head: "the Zollman effect gets weaker in larger networks when using standard [evidence] updating, but gets stronger in larger communities if agents update by pooling functions instead."
- **Source:** Zollman 2013 "Network Epistemology: Communication in Epistemic Communities" (Philosophy Compass); kevinzollman.com/uploads/.../netepist.pdf; pooling-comparison from network-epistemology pooling-function work surfaced via Semantic Scholar
- **Confidence:** high

### Claim: Formal epistemology already has the sharp theorem that pooling and learning do not commute, except for geometric pooling — so "pooling ≠ evaluating evidence" is theorem-level established
- **Evidence:** "Linear pooling is not externally Bayesian — updating each expert's prior with data and aggregating their posteriors yields a different result than first combining experts' priors and then updating the aggregated prior. In contrast, geometric averaging rules allow group credences to commute with conditionalization... A pooling function is full-revelation Bayes-compatible if and only if it is geometric." This is exactly the formal content of "pool opinions" vs. "update on the evidence."
- **Source:** "Learning and Pooling, Pooling and Learning" (Erkenntnis, forthcoming); "Support for Geometric Pooling" (Review of Symbolic Logic), cambridge.org/.../D02CBB4FD9D9A400E46EE31B3E5806AF
- **Confidence:** high

### Claim: Bayesian agents who evaluate others' evidence against their OWN beliefs (trusting evidence less when beliefs differ) lock into stable, evidence-proof polarization — a direct formal precedent for "local own-model evaluation → persistent divergence"
- **Evidence:** O'Connor & Weatherall's polarization mechanism "involves treating evidence generated by other agents as uncertain when their beliefs are relatively different from one's own... they distrust evidence from those who hold different beliefs from their own." Result: "this sort of polarization can be very stable: in some cases, no amount of evidence from scientists with the correct beliefs will be able to move the credences of the scientists who are in error... [agents] form increasingly distant beliefs until they no longer influence one another." This is a Bayesian, evaluate-against-own-model analogue of bounded confidence.
- **Source:** O'Connor & Weatherall 2018, "Scientific Polarization," arxiv.org/abs/1712.04561; ssrn.com/abstract=3098608
- **Confidence:** high

### Claim: Heterogeneous MISSPECIFIED Bayesian models give a closed-form criterion for *entrenched disagreement* — persistent divergence from local updating is fully characterized
- **Evidence:** Bohren & Hauser provide "a simple criterion to characterize long-run beliefs based on the underlying form of misspecification," and "the characterization applies to settings with model heterogeneity and provides conditions for entrenched disagreement," covering partisan bias, overreaction, naive learning, level-k. They also flag when "a representative agent approach is valid" — i.e. when heterogeneity does/does not wash out. This is the heterogeneous-model persistent-disagreement literature the paper currently omits.
- **Source:** Bohren & Hauser 2021, "Learning With Heterogeneous Misspecified Models: Characterization and Robustness," Econometrica 89(6):3025–3077; onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.3982/ECTA15318
- **Confidence:** high

### Claim: Hegselmann–Krause bounded confidence is the opinion-dynamics statement of "local-only evaluation → persistent fragmentation," and heterogeneity makes the outcome non-monotone (sometimes more divergence, sometimes re-entrant consensus)
- **Evidence:** In HK, "an individual tends to trust only opinions that do not differ from its own by more than r... interactions are limited to be effective only between individuals whose opinions are already close enough," and "every HK process... converges in finite time to a stabilised opinion profile of opinion clusters" — persistent disagreement. With heterogeneous bounds: "the phase diagram reveals a highly complex and nonmonotonous behaviour, with a re-entrant consensus phase in the region where fragmentation... is expected for the homogeneous case." (Mirrors the project's own "heterogeneity sometimes washes out, sometimes doesn't" findings.)
- **Source:** Hegselmann & Krause 2002 (J. Artificial Societies and Social Simulation); Lorenz "Heterogeneous bounds of confidence: Meet, Discuss and Find Consensus!" arxiv.org/pdf/0801.1399
- **Confidence:** high

### Claim: Lehrer–Wagner is the canonical POOLING / forced-consensus pole — iterated weighted averaging that provably converges to consensus — and is the clean foil for the paper's "we do NOT pool" claim, with a known critique literature
- **Evidence:** "The Lehrer–Wagner method is justified by Wagner's Theorem... any method satisfying Irrelevance of Alternatives and Zero Unanimity must be weighted averaging." It "formulat[es] a model of convergence toward consensus applied to an allocation matrix of vectors." Critiques: Forrest's "zero weight problem" (Synthese 1985); Bradley argues "the model is unsatisfactory from the point of view of rational belief revision and cannot be used to support the claim that linear averaging is robust in the face of revision" (the non-commutativity objection again); Baird; Braaten (argumentation vs. averaging).
- **Source:** Lehrer & Wagner 1981, *Rational Consensus in Science and Society*; Wagner "Rationality as weighted averaging" (Synthese), link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01064700; Bradley "Reaching a Consensus," personal.lse.ac.uk/bradleyr/pdf/consensus(SCW).pdf
- **Confidence:** high

### Claim: The specific NOVEL contribution that survives prior-art check is structure-valued divergence (local testing of a shared STRUCTURAL/Bayes-net revision → structural incommensurability), which the precedents do not formalize
- **Evidence:** Every precedent above produces divergence in *credence over a fixed hypothesis space* — bandit arms (Zollman), a scalar/partisan state (Acemoglu–Ozdaglar, Bohren–Hauser), a real-valued opinion (HK), or pooled probability vectors (Lehrer–Wagner). None formalize agents who locally test a proposed *change to generative structure* (an edge/wiring revision in a Bayes net) against their own model and end up with persistently *different structures*. The "platform influence" heterogeneous-prior work even notes that "agents with access to underlying signals can sometimes overcome prior disagreement, but when restricted to averaging neighbors' opinions alone, heterogeneous starting points become entrenched" — confirming the pool-vs-data axis is mapped, while leaving structure-valued divergence open. (Caveat: the project's own operator-set-Fisher finding shows the *current model* often expresses paradigm difference in MEANS not edges — so this novelty must be demonstrated, not assumed.)
- **Source:** Candogan, Immorlica, Light & Anunrojwong 2022, "Social Learning under Platform Influence," arxiv.org/pdf/2202.12453; synthesis across the above; internal caveat from project memory (operator-set Fisher / theory-id-in-means)
- **Confidence:** med

## Open threads
- **Novelty verdict, stated plainly:** the *distinction* (local data-evaluation vs. opinion pooling) does NOT survive as novel — it is the backbone of network epistemology and has a commutativity theorem in formal epistemology. The *phenomenon* (heterogeneous local evaluation → persistent divergence/polarization) does NOT survive as novel either — O'Connor–Weatherall, Bohren–Hauser, HK all have it. The paper must reframe: position pooling-vs-evaluation as *inherited framing* and claim novelty only on **structure-valued / Bayes-net-structural incommensurability**.
- Did not retrieve the *exact* Acemoglu–Ozdaglar 2011 "heterogeneous priors → persistent disagreement" theorem text (only secondary description); worth a direct read of Acemoglu, Como, Fagnani & Ozdaglar 2013 "Opinion fluctuations and disagreement in social networks" and the 2011 *Review of Economic Studies* Bayesian-learning piece before citing specific results.
- Fudenberg-style misspecified-learning (Fudenberg, Lanzani, Strack; Esponda–Pouzo "Berk–Nash equilibrium") was not retrieved this pass — it is the single-agent/equilibrium analogue of Bohren–Hauser and would strengthen the "misspecified model → wrong but locally-confirmed belief" lineage. Recommend a follow-up query.
- The clean rhetorical move: contrast Lehrer–Wagner (forced averaging → consensus) vs. Zollman/Bala–Goyal (Bayes on shared data → can still herd/diverge) vs. THIS paper (Bayes on shared *structural revision* → structural incommensurability). That triangulation is defensible and honestly credited.
