# What the four-variable experiment suite covers

This audit uses the submitted figure numbering in `IWAI_26_Paradigm/main.tex`. The new running example has candidate-specific latent commitments P/O and the shared observable commitments C, G and M. The incumbent graph is `P -> C,G,M`; the rival is `O -> C,G; G -> M`. Both candidates are supplied in advance. The observation operator reads C, G and M directly.

“Run all experiments” here means the retained candidate-inference experiments and their relevant controls on this new world. It does **not** mean that every historical figure has been rerun with four variables. Several figures depict unused theory, a separate retrospective-reliability algorithm, or a utility-dependent graph-editing prototype. Those require distinct methods and cannot be recreated merely by changing the candidate constructor.

The exact five-coordinate compression developed earlier was a mathematical reduction of the ten-variable model. This four-variable proposal is a **new, interpretable experimental world** with different candidate CPDs and observations. It preserves the selected inference mechanisms, not exact historical trajectories. The independent-case covariance-learning suite remains a separate proposal and supplies no results for this four-variable assessment.

## Mechanisms covered by the present run plan

| Mechanism or experimental claim | Direct check in this suite | What would exceed its scope |
|---|---|---|
| Posterior selection after an imposed observation change | Cycle, no-change and no-forgetting runs; retained log odds and entropy | Discovery of an unrepresented theory; recovery of the causal DAG that generated independent cases |
| Adaptation versus information loss under repeated sharing | Candidate-consistent post-change world with selective evidence, paired sharing controls and a fuller-information reference observer | Calling a persistent erroneous preference an architectural necessity without showing what information each comparator receives |
| Gaussian compromise versus retained candidate alternatives | K=1 and K=2 conditions with stated priors and memory; means as well as model probabilities | “Only representation differs” when anchors, forgetting, latent semantics or evidence access differ |
| Delay from restricted evidence | Diagnostic attention endpoints 0, .01 and 1 on the complete graph | Treating .01 as no direct evidence; interpreting prescribed weights as learned motivation or active experiment choice |
| Information exchange through a minority | Hypothesis pooling on/off and both sharing routes off, paired within each scenario | Calling `alpha_m=0` complete isolation while Gaussian parameter fusion remains active |
| Network/placement sensitivity | Four fixed graphs plus informed hub versus rim on the same wheel | Separating density, degree-based influence, path length and communication cost with a four-graph montage |
| Persistent disagreement under selective evidence | Camp means, camp candidate probabilities, global/common log-odds drift and source-gap diagnostics | Inferring distinct paradigm choice from a mean-distance plateau, or inferring a genuine steady state from a finite trace |
| Source-driven versus unforced log-odds differences | Continue an existing state after disabling observation weights; verify the expected pooling recurrence | Interpreting the continuation as a new world change; claiming it destroys a schism if both camps already chose the same model |
| Belief utility, structural proposals and retrospective reliability | Not executed | Presenting their old figures or equations as tested components of these runs |

All inference runs must use the accepted order of model-score pooling, within-frame Gaussian fusion, scoring before deposit, weighted information update, and anchored forgetting. Changing the world is authorized here; silently changing that inference order would be a different experiment.

## Important consequences of the new observation interface

The former ten-variable world included a separately noisy relational row, `calx - mass - gas`. The new operator has three direct rows selecting C, G and M. Consequently its sensory Fisher contribution

\[
J=H^\top\operatorname{diag}(w)H/\sigma_o^2
\]

is diagonal in the four-coordinate basis. Observations update means and marginal information, but do not directly deposit off-diagonal information. Candidate CPDs remain genuinely different, and predictive scores can distinguish their beliefs. That is not the same as learning new dependence edges from a relational observation. Within-frame sharing and reference-prior relaxation must not be described as evidence-driven graph discovery.

The world supplies a prescribed commitment vector and measurement noise. Even a post-change vector consistent with the rival's mean is not a sequence of fresh latent cases generated from that rival's full DAG. Therefore an observed predictive KL calculation diagnoses separation between the supplied current/prior predictions; it does not turn these runs into a causal-network recovery study. Covariance and mean differences should be reported separately wherever the paper claims specifically structural discrimination.

P and O have different candidate meanings. Parameter fusion inside a frame is well-defined; averaging the full four-dimensional incumbent and rival priors would also average P with O. The K=1 comparator should instead use the **Gaussian marginal over the shared observables C,G,M**, with an independent unused dummy coordinate only if the array interface requires four dimensions. Marginalization requires the observable covariance block (or the Schur complement of latent precision), not a principal precision submatrix. This defines a fair shared measurement space, but is a newly specified comparator; it must not be described as byte-identical to averaging the original full candidate networks.

Core-cost indices such as \(\kappa=T\mathbf1\) are also coordinate- and unit-dependent. Neither the earlier normalized aggregate nor the new P/O coordinate conventions preserve every original centrality value automatically. If a cost figure or readout remains, define its operator, input perturbation and units explicitly. It is not needed to execute the retained candidate-inference experiments.

## Minimal checks that complete the coverage

These are targeted checks of the claims above, not a request to rebuild the removed prototype.

1. **Construction and scoring contracts.** Positive-definite candidate priors; stated means/covariances; direct H with no latent observation; correct score-before-deposit order; exact K=1 handling. Independently compare observable predictive distributions, separating mean and covariance contributions.
2. **Label invariance.** Swapping the two candidate arrays, reference priors and initial model weights must swap the output probabilities without changing observable predictions. This catches index-specific oxygen/incumbent behaviour.
3. **Indistinguishable candidates.** Two identical candidate distributions initialized with equal weights must stay equally weighted, even under heterogeneous attention and social sharing. A stronger observational-equivalence check can vary an entirely unobserved, independent latent coordinate while leaving observable marginals identical.
4. **Attention endpoints.** At zero weight, a diagnostic row must contribute no candidate-specific score or information. Compare complete attention, a small nonzero trickle and zero direct diagnostic information rather than infer one condition from another. Zero G/M weights do not guarantee zero *total* model evidence after an earlier observation history: C may have different conditional predictive distributions under the adapted candidates. A completely source-free continuation zeros every observation row.
5. **Readout verification.** Report mean disagreement together with soft candidate probabilities and log odds. Distinguish within-agent entropy from disagreement between agents. Preserve explicit non-crossings; state whether a transition is a first crossing or sustained crossing.
6. **Paired adaptation controls.** For the candidate-consistent post-change experiment, use the same underlying noisy observations across communication conditions and the same initial states when isolating a post-change sharing intervention. Report any full-information reference's extra access explicitly; it is not an equal-data-budget competitor.

Sensitivity over coupling/noise, a reversal back to the incumbent, additional graph realizations and prior-precision sweeps would further bound generalization. They are warranted if corresponding broad claims remain, but are not prerequisites for reporting a finite, explicitly configured illustration. A demonstration of a supplied candidate's successful revision does not require promising a universal spectral or asymptotic theorem.

## Completed independent validation

The numerical receipt is [INDEPENDENT_VALIDATION.json](INDEPENDENT_VALIDATION.json), which records the world and recovered-engine source hashes, package versions, settings and maximum errors. These checks run separately from the population assessment in float64. They do not tune the population model.

The prior observable covariances are

\[
\Sigma_P=\begin{pmatrix}
.66&-.16&-.16\\
-.16&.66&.16\\
-.16&.16&.66
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Sigma_O=\begin{pmatrix}
.66&.16&.144\\
.16&.66&.594\\
.144&.594&1.0346
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Their means are `(1,-1,-1)` and `(1,+1,+1)`. Marginalizing the explanatory coordinate genuinely gives different dependence patterns: the noiseless observable prior has `Cov(C,M | G) = −.121212` in the incumbent and zero, to rounding, in the rival. This is a check of the supplied Gaussian priors. Adding independent observation noise can alter observed conditional-independence relations, and the repeated fixed-pattern experiment does not identify arbitrary causal directions.

For the initial predictive distributions, the Gaussian KL separates into a mean-difference term and a covariance term. The latter is the KL that remains after matching the means; the former is evaluated using the target distribution's covariance. These are analytic decompositions, not additional population recovery experiments.

| Sensor standard deviation | Direction | Mean contribution | Covariance contribution | Total KL, nats |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| .5 | Incumbent to rival | 2.54973 | .19086 | 2.74059 |
| .5 | Rival to incumbent | 3.94580 | .23145 | 4.17725 |
| 1 | Incumbent to rival | 1.67713 | .05934 | 1.73647 |
| 1 | Rival to incumbent | 2.23569 | .07115 | 2.30684 |

At the cycle noise level of 1, approximately 97% of the initial KL separation comes from the mean difference. At noise `.5`, the fraction is approximately 93–94%. Thus the supplied priors are predictively distinguishable, but successful main-run selection cannot be attributed specifically to wiring. A matched-means/variance model-recovery study would be required for that stronger claim; it is not needed if the paper accurately describes selection among structurally and empirically different supplied explanations.

The fixed-pattern observation world also permits an initial expected-score check. In the mixed world `G=-1, M=+1` at noise `.5`, the expected rival-minus-incumbent log score is `−2.19780` for a G-only observer and `+2.06548` for an M-only observer. Their equal-camp average is approximately `−.06616` nats per step. The mixed-world full-observation reference initially favours the incumbent (`−.94258` nats per step). This is not perfectly balanced evidence. In the candidate-consistent post-change pattern `G=M=+1`, both single-channel expected scores favour the rival. These are **initial-prior calculations**, not stationary evidence-source predictions after inference, fusion and forgetting.

Five independent inference controls passed through the recovered rival engine using four agents, 32 rounds, heterogeneous attention, a connected trust matrix, omega `.9`, pooling `.02` and a prescribed change at round 13:

| Check | Maximum probability discrepancy | Outcome |
|---|---:|---|
| Swap candidate order, priors and initial weights | 0 | Probabilities and final candidate states swap exactly |
| Duplicate identical candidates | 0 | Equal initial probabilities remain `.5` |
| Same observable priors, different independent unobserved latent priors | 0 | The latent-only difference supplies no model evidence |
| Original candidates, C-only attention from equal initial weights | `3.55e-15` | Coincident C priors remain observationally indistinguishable in this control |
| All observation weights zero | `3.89e-15` | Probabilities match the analytically iterated pure log-weight pooling rule |

The C-only control starts without an earlier diagnostic history; it does not imply that C is always nondiagnostic after G/M evidence has changed the candidates. The source-off control uses nonuniform initial model probabilities and checks their pooling evolution rather than merely checking that `.5` stays `.5`.
