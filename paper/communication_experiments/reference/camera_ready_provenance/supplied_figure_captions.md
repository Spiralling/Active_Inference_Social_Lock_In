(b) A magnified agent retains conditional beliefs rho_ik and probabilities q_ik over supplied accounts. **fig02_precision_pool**

Equal-weight Gaussian pooling combines existing precision entries. The inputs are the exact observable marginal priors on C, G and M in the revised release and oxygen candidates; hidden P and O coordinates are integrated out before comparison. Undirected links are labelled by off-diagonal precision entries (blue solid: negative; orange dashed: positive), rounded to three decimals. Diagonal entries and information vectors are omitted from the drawing but included in the single-Gaussian comparator. The G--M entry present only in the oxygen input is halved; opposing C--M entries nearly cancel. In general, entries zero in both inputs remain zero. These are conditional-dependence links, not the directed causal arrows of the candidate graphs. This closed-form illustration is not a simulation.

**fig03_camp_distance**

Different update recipes retain different parameter distances, without sustained opposing candidate choices. Two camps of eight agents observe G or M in the oxygen-reference world on a complete contact graph. D_t is the Euclidean distance between camp means on C,G,M; the rival condition uses each agent's highest-probability candidate. Both axes are logarithmic; all positive values are shown without clipping. Memory values are given in the legend; prior anchors also differ, so this is not an isolated test of representation. Both camps in the represented-rival condition adopt oxygen. Lines average 20 seeds; ribbons are pointwise 95% percentile-bootstrap intervals for the seed mean (2,000 resamples of complete seed curves). No smoothing or interval clipping is used.

**fig04_cycle**

The represented-rival transition passes through uncertainty before oxygen gains support. Green shows population-mean oxygen probability (left axis); grey fill shows posterior entropy computed per agent before averaging (right axis, nats; maximum log 2). Narrow ribbons around both curves are pointwise 95% percentile-bootstrap intervals for the seed mean (50 seeds; 2,000 resamples of complete seed curves), not min--max ranges. The vertical dotted line marks the first changed-world update at step 80; C and G stay +1 while M changes from -1 to +1. Sixteen agents communicate on a complete graph, with memory .9, model-score sharing .02 and sensor noise standard deviation 1. The first point follows the first update. As in the original composition, the orange span marks post-change mean individual entropy above 0.35 nats, here steps 100--188. This inherited display threshold is a descriptive guide, not a fitted or validated crisis boundary. The entropy axis ends at the binary maximum log 2, rather than the original 1.1 nats. No smoothing or clipping is applied. The main text reports the unchanged-world and memory controls.

**fig06_topology**

Contact topology changes adoption timing, with incomplete adoption on the cycle. All 24 nodes and edges are shown; black outlines mark four full-attention agents. After the world changes at step 80, the other 20 agents assign weight 0.01 to gas-uptake and mass-change observations; combustion remains fully attended. Thin lines show every restricted agent from seed 0; heavy lines average restricted agents within each seed and then average seeds. The graph definitions, informed positions and row-normalized self-plus-neighbour weights match the accepted experiment. Memory is 0.9 and model-score sharing 0.02. Lambda2 is the unnormalized graph Laplacian's second eigenvalue. Density and source placement vary alongside topology; this is not an isolated test of that eigenvalue. On the cycle, 0 of 50 seeds reach a restricted-group mean above 0.9 within 600 updates; noncrossing runs are right-censored, not assigned a crossing at the horizon. Lines average 50 seeds; ribbons are pointwise 95% percentile-bootstrap intervals for the seed mean (2,000 resamples of complete seed curves). No smoothing or interval clipping is used.

**fig_model**

The supplied combustion explanations use distinct hidden variables. (a) Combustion extent C predicts phlogiston release P and gas uptake G; P predicts decreased solid mass M. (b) C predicts oxygen incorporation O, which predicts both G and increased M. Dashed nodes are unobserved. Every prior regression coefficient has magnitude .8 and the indicated sign. Variables are standardized schematic quantities, not calibrated chemistry. Both candidates predict shared observables C,G,M, with reference means (1,1,-1) and (1,1,1); P and O are never averaged across candidates. The main simulations change the fixed mass signal, while the separate matched-marginal check tests dependence discrimination.

**fig_adaptation**

Social sharing allows gas observers to adopt oxygen, while their isolated counterparts retain the release account over the observed horizon. At step 80 the world changes to the oxygen reference pattern. Agents branch from one shared pre-change state into networked selective observation, isolated selective observation, or a reference with both diagnostic channels available individually. (a,b) Oxygen probabilities for the gas- and mass-observing camps during the first 500 of 1,500 updates; reference agents in both plotted groups observe both channels. Points in (c,d) average the last 375 updates within each seed; whiskers use the same whole-seed bootstrap as the ribbons. (c) Mean squared error of the posterior mixture mean on C,G,M against the noiseless world vector, averaged over coordinates and agents within each seed. Sensor noise variance is excluded from this error; it is not a held-out log-likelihood. (d) Negative log density of the full posterior predictive mixture on four independent withheld observations per agent and update, in nats per three-coordinate observation. Lower is better in (c,d); the NLL axis is expanded to show differences. The reference is not a centralized observer pooling all agents' observations. These data test adaptation to a prescribed mean pattern, not recovery of the true causal wiring. Lines average 20 seeds; ribbons are pointwise 95% percentile-bootstrap intervals for the seed mean (2,000 resamples of complete seed curves). No smoothing or interval clipping is used.

**fig_identifiability**

Dependence alone can distinguish the supplied candidates under a fixed known-model classifier. (a,b) Observable correlation matrices of the generating models before measurement noise. Both models have zero means and equal observable marginal variances; the release model's G residual variance is adjusted to 0.5448 for this check only. (c) Fraction choosing the generating candidate after the indicated numbers of independent full observations, for measurement-noise standard deviations sigma. Each candidate generates 200 independent sequences. Lines join measured sample counts; ribbons are pointwise 95% percentile-bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples of seed indices, retaining both source candidates). A product-of-marginals classifier stays at 0.5; posterior ties count half-correct. The classifier has fixed known Gaussian parameters, equal model priors and no social pooling or parameter adaptation. This is a discrimination check for this two-model menu, not graph discovery or a structure-only ablation of the adaptive simulations. Both edge patterns and coefficient signs contribute to the differing dependence.
